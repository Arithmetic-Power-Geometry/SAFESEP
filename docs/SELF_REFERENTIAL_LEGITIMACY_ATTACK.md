## Candidate
Evidence E needed to justify policy P is governed by P itself. Without an independently grounded seed, least-fixed-point closure contains neither P nor E. With an external P seed, closure obtains E and the remaining decision can resolve.

## Diagnostic
The controlled matched family preserves q at cost 1. At 200 worlds q leaves 9,900 decision-incompatible pairs; at 10,000 worlds it leaves 24,995,000.

## Prior-art boundary
This formulation does not survive the novelty test. Automated trust negotiation has long studied sensitive credentials governed by disclosure policies, including cyclic dependencies where neither party releases the next credential. Published work explicitly discusses cyclic credential/policy dependencies and methods for detecting or handling them. Other trust-management work uses recursive credential retrieval and safeguards against cyclic-dependency livelock.

Therefore the unseeded P/E fixed-point obstruction is retained as a reproducible collision result rather than a breakthrough claim.

## Next target
A stronger construction should match dependency graph, SCCs, least-fixed-point closure, costs and ordinary information partitions while differing in safe separability because justification-rule validity is world-dependent. It must then be compared with nonmonotonic authorization logics, policy-combining semantics, epistemic planning and trust negotiation.

## Empirical boundary
The 10,000-world data are a controlled theorem stress test, not production IAM telemetry. Public authorization datasets do not natively provide counterfactual rule-legitimacy worlds and protected-evidence dependencies, so those labels are not fabricated.
