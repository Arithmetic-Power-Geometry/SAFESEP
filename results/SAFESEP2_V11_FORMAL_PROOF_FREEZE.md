# SAFESEP-II V11 formal proof freeze — dynamic behavioral kernel

## Model assumptions

The result below is restricted to the explicit finite SAFESEP-II model satisfying all of the following.

1. **Finite explicit worlds.** The current compatible set C is finite.
2. **Decision sufficiency.** Resolution terminates when D is constant on C; exact world identification is unnecessary.
3. **Deterministic observations.** Each one-shot probe e has a fixed outcome O_e(w) in each world.
4. **One-shot probes.** Once executed, a probe is removed from the unused set U.
5. **World-independent authority effects.** Req(e), Grant(e), and Revoke(e) do not depend on the true world or observed outcome.
6. **Set-valued authority.** If current authority is A, executing e yields
       A' = (A union Grant(e)) \ Revoke(e).
   Hence repeating the same authority effect is idempotent.
7. **No hidden side effects.** Apart from observation refinement, authority update, and removal from U, probe execution does not alter D, future outcome maps, or probe semantics.

The theorem must not be exported beyond these assumptions.

## Definitions

For compatible worlds C define the unresolved decision obligations
    P(C) = {{u,v} subset C : D(u) != D(v)}.

For unused probes U define the relevant authority tokens
    R(U) = union_{e in U} Req(e).

Let W(P) be the worlds occurring as endpoints of pairs in P.

At normalized state Q=(P,A,U), define the behavioral type of e in U by

    tau_Q(e) = (
        Req(e) cap R(U),
        Grant(e) cap R(U),
        Revoke(e) cap R(U),
        Pi_{W(P)}(e)
    ),

where Pi is the observation partition induced by equality of O_e on W(P).

## Lemma 1 — decision-obligation quotient

Resolve(C,A,U) depends on C only through P(C).

### Proof
P(C)=empty iff D is constant on C, exactly the terminal condition. For a legitimate probe e and outcome o, an incompatible pair {u,v} survives in the child iff both endpoints produce o. Thus the child obligation set is determined from P and e alone. Authority transition and unused-probe update are independent of discarded same-decision worlds. Induction on |U| gives equality of the world-state and obligation-state recursions. QED.

## Lemma 2 — irrelevant-authority erasure

Replacing A by A cap R(U) preserves resolvability.

### Proof
Every future executability test has the form Req(e) subset A for e in U, and Req(e) subset R(U). Tokens outside R(U) occur in no future requirement. Their presence, grant, or revocation cannot alter future executability. After each execution project again to the new R(U'). Induction on |U| proves the claim. QED.

## Lemma 3 — duplicate behavioral probes are interchangeable at Q

If e,f in U satisfy tau_Q(e)=tau_Q(f), then any first-step transition generated by e has a quotient-equivalent first-step transition generated by f.

### Proof
Their requirement components coincide on R(U), and all requirements lie in R(U), so e is executable iff f is executable.

Their observation partitions on W(P) coincide, so they induce the same partition of unresolved obligations, up to outcome-label renaming.

Their grants and revocations coincide on R(U), so after relevance projection their authority successors coincide.

Finally, executing either removes one member of the same current behavioral class. The other remains. Therefore corresponding children agree on P and projected A; their remaining probe multisets differ only by the names of interchangeable representatives. QED.

## Lemma 4 — one representative of a duplicate type suffices

Deleting all but one probe from each behavioral type at Q preserves safe resolvability.

### Proof by induction on the number of unused probes
The terminal case is immediate.

Assume a resolving policy exists in the original state. If its first probe is retained, compare corresponding children. If its first probe was a deleted duplicate f, substitute the retained representative e. Lemma 3 gives quotient-equivalent children.

A possible concern is that both e and f occur later on one path. After the first is executed, the second has the same deterministic observation restricted to every descendant subset, so it cannot create a refinement unavailable from the first partition. Its set-valued authority update is the same idempotent map on currently relevant tokens. Thus a later execution of the duplicate cannot be essential solely by repeating the same behavior. Recompute behavioral types at each child; additional types may merge but no deleted duplicate becomes distinguishable from its retained representative on that child. Apply the induction hypothesis.

Conversely, every policy using the deduplicated set is also a valid policy in the original set. Hence resolvability is preserved. QED.

## Theorem — dynamic behavioral-kernel exactness

Under assumptions 1–7, recursively:
1. replace C by P(C);
2. replace A by A cap R(U);
3. retain one representative per current behavioral type;
4. after every observation branch, recompute P, R, and behavioral types.

This dynamic quotient preserves exact safe resolvability.

### Proof
Combine Lemmas 1–4 recursively. QED.

## Parameterized consequence

Let k=|P| and r=|R(U)| at the initial normalized state.

At most 2k worlds occur as endpoints of P. A deterministic observation induces a partition of those endpoints, giving at most Bell(2k) observation-partition signatures.

Each of requirement, grant, and revoke has at most 2^r signatures. Therefore the number of behavioral types at a normalized state is bounded by

    T(k,r) <= Bell(2k) * 2^(3r).

After deduplication, the number of represented probes is bounded by T(k,r), independent of the original named probe count m.

A brute-force memoized search over normalized obligation subsets, projected authority subsets, and subsets of the bounded type universe is therefore bounded by a computable function of k+r, after polynomial-time scanning/canonicalization of the explicit input probes.

### Corollary candidate
Restricted deterministic SAFESEP-II resolvability is fixed-parameter tractable parameterized by k+r.

## Remaining proof obligation before manuscript theorem status

The proof above is now explicit enough to audit, but the FPT corollary needs one additional parameter-monotonicity check: along descendants, the relevant token universe cannot introduce a token outside the initial R(U), and obligation endpoints cannot introduce a world outside the initial W(P). Both appear immediate because U only shrinks and P only loses pairs, but this must be stated formally in the final theorem.

The computational batteries are falsification evidence, not part of the mathematical proof.

## Claim discipline

Do not claim:
- FPT for stochastic probes;
- FPT for reusable probes;
- FPT for counters/quantitative resources;
- FPT for world-dependent grants/revocations;
- FPT for hidden/destructive state changes outside authority sets;
- novelty of action equivalence, belief-state compression, or parameterized planning in general.

The potentially novel object is the exact decision-obligation + relevant-authority + dynamic behavioral-probe kernel for revocable authorization-sensitive evidence acquisition.
